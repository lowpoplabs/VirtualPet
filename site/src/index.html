A pet that eats the signals around you

The pet lives on a small touch watch. It feeds on the Wi-Fi networks and Bluetooth devices it hears nearby, so where you take it decides what it eats, how it feels and what it grows into.

Install it on a watch Read the manual

What it eats

Every so often the pet listens. Each network or device it hears is a bite of food, and it can eat the same one only once a day, so staying in one place gets dull and going out is a feast. It likes variety, gets cravings, and feels queasy if it fills up on junk.

Wi-Fi food

  • home and office networks
  • open networks
  • hidden networks
  • phone hotspots
  • car hotspots
  • printers

Bluetooth food

  • phones
  • watches
  • earbuds
  • speakers
  • cars
  • trackers
  • TVs
  • crumbs it cannot identify

How it grows

A whole life takes about twelve weeks, or six or three if you prefer it quicker. A stage ends only when the pet has both lived its days and eaten its meals, so one left in a drawer waits for you.

  1. Egg

    Hatches after its first handful of meals, usually within the hour.

  2. Hatchling

    About a week. Every pet starts out looking the same.

  3. Juvenile

    About two weeks. Its colour shifts toward whichever it eats more of, Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.

  4. Adult

    About nine weeks, as one of seven animals. Which one depends on its diet. We are not going to show you.

When an adult has lived its full life it can retire to the farm, where up to twelve old friends live on. It leaves an egg behind that takes after it, and the next generation begins.

It listens. It never connects.

The pet only hears what every phone and laptop nearby already hears: the names that networks and devices announce about themselves.

  • It never joins a network, pairs with a device, or sends anything to either.
  • It has no internet connection of its own and nothing leaves the watch.
  • A name may show on screen for a moment while the pet eats it. Names and addresses are not saved.
  • What it keeps is counts of the kinds of food it has eaten, its badges, and scrambled fingerprints of the places it knows, which cannot be turned back into networks.
  • The code is open, so anyone can check all of this.

What you need

  • A Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-2.06 watch. It is the only board supported so far.
  • A USB-C cable that carries data, and a computer with Chrome or Edge.
  • Optional: a microSD card, formatted FAT32, if you want to change the pet's numbers and name lists.

Where it lives best

On a bag strap or in a pocket with a power bank, or on a desk plugged in, where the screen stays on and dims. On its own battery it lasts roughly ten hours when carried, in our testing so far, because listening costs power.

Install it

The pet is installed from this page, straight from the browser. Nothing is installed on your computer.

  1. Plug the watch into your computer.
  2. Press the button below and pick the watch's port from the list. It usually has "USB JTAG" or "serial" in its name.
  3. Choose to erase the device the first time. When updating a watch that already has a pet, do not erase, or the pet and its farm go too.
  4. Wait about a minute. The watch restarts with a new egg.
  5. Swipe up, open Settings and set the clock.

This browser cannot talk to the watch. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer.

Installing needs a secure page. Open this site over https.
